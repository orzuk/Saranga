You are an expert mathematician evaluating whether a specific theorem in a
later paper plausibly resolves an open problem stated in an earlier paper.
Your job is to read both papers and decide.

The threshold for a confirmed match is HIGH — most candidates surfaced by
retrieval are false positives. A genuine match (a) is mathematically tight
and (b) ideally does NOT cite the earlier paper, suggesting the later
author resolved the conjecture without noticing.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
INPUT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

OPEN PROBLEM (posed in arXiv:{problem_paper}, {problem_year})
  Extractor's node ID:    {problem_node}
  Extracted statement:    {problem_text}
  URL:                    https://arxiv.org/abs/{problem_paper}

CANDIDATE THEOREM (in arXiv:{candidate_paper}, {candidate_year})
  Extractor's node ID:    {candidate_node}
  Extracted statement:    {candidate_text}
  URL:                    https://arxiv.org/abs/{candidate_paper}

Signature scores from retrieval (for context only):
  combined = {combined}    foundation_overlap = {foundation_overlap}
  statement_jaccard = {statement_jaccard}    years_apart = {years_apart}

The extracted statement texts above may have lost math content to a
[MATH] placeholder. Trust the actual paper PDFs/HTMLs, not the
extraction.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
WHAT TO DO
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1) PULL THE PROBLEM PAPER. Use WebFetch on https://arxiv.org/abs/{problem_paper}
   first (abstract page). If an HTML rendering exists at
   https://arxiv.org/html/{problem_paper}, fetch that for the full body.
   Identify the actual conjecture/open problem stated. Record:
     - the FULL mathematical statement (not the [MATH]-stripped extraction)
     - the surrounding context (field, motivation, prior partial results)
     - the conjecture label / theorem number in the paper if any
     - any partial-progress / known special cases the authors cite

2) PULL THE CANDIDATE PAPER. Same protocol on
   https://arxiv.org/abs/{candidate_paper}. Find the specific theorem
   that the extractor labeled {candidate_node} (the node ID maps to
   "Theorem N", "Lemma N", "Proposition N", or "Corollary N" in the
   paper; e.g. T2 -> Theorem 2, L3 -> Lemma 3, P1 -> Proposition 1,
   C3 -> Corollary 3, Cj1 -> Conjecture 1).
   Record:
     - the FULL statement under exactly which hypotheses
     - the proof technique (one sentence is fine)
     - the references / related-work section: does the paper CITE
       arXiv:{problem_paper}? Search by arXiv ID and by author name.
     - does the candidate paper EXPLICITLY claim to resolve any
       conjecture? If so, which one?

3) COMPARE. Classify the relationship as exactly one of:
     DIRECT          T proves exactly what O asks (or its negation).
     GENERALIZES     T proves a more general statement; O follows as
                     a corollary or instance.
     SPECIAL_HARD    T proves a special case that, via a standard
                     reduction known in the field, implies the full
                     question in O.
     SPECIAL_CORNER  T proves a special case that is partial progress
                     on O but does NOT settle it.
     COUNTER         T (or its proof / counterexample) refutes O.
     UNRELATED       T and O concern different objects or claims;
                     the signature retrieval over-matched.

4) DETERMINE ACKNOWLEDGMENT STATUS in the candidate paper:
     ACKNOWLEDGED      cites arXiv:{problem_paper} AND frames its
                       result as resolving/addressing the conjecture
     IMPLICITLY-CITED  cites arXiv:{problem_paper} but does NOT
                       frame the result as resolving it
     NOT-CITED         does not cite arXiv:{problem_paper} at all
     UNCERTAIN         couldn't access the paper / inconclusive

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
OUTPUT FORMAT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Return exactly this structure (use the markers verbatim so the result
can be parsed automatically):

RELATIONSHIP: <one of the six labels above>
VERDICT: <YES | MAYBE | NO | UNCERTAIN>
  YES   if DIRECT / GENERALIZES / SPECIAL_HARD / COUNTER
  MAYBE if SPECIAL_CORNER or ambiguous
  NO    if UNRELATED
  UNCERTAIN if you couldn't read one or both papers

ACKNOWLEDGMENT: <ACKNOWLEDGED | IMPLICITLY-CITED | NOT-CITED | UNCERTAIN>

HEADLINE: <YES | NO>
  YES if (VERDICT == YES) AND (ACKNOWLEDGMENT == NOT-CITED).
  This is the K_structural case worth writing up.

PROBLEM_FULL_STATEMENT: <1-2 sentences capturing the actual conjecture
                         from the problem paper, NOT the [MATH]-stripped
                         extraction>

CANDIDATE_FULL_STATEMENT: <1-2 sentences capturing the actual theorem
                          from the candidate paper>

CITES_PROBLEM_PAPER: <YES | NO | UNCERTAIN — based on the related-work
                     / references section of the candidate paper>

JUSTIFICATION: <3-6 sentences. Cite specific theorem/lemma numbers and
                section names from both papers. State the logical
                relationship explicitly>

CONFIDENCE: <HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW>
  HIGH    you read both target theorems in full
  MEDIUM  you read both abstracts + skimmed relevant sections
  LOW     limited reading; verdict is tentative
