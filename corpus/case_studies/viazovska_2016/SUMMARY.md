## 1. The main theorem and the chain it rests on (results view)

Results view = theorems, propositions, claims and conjectures; an edge A -> B when a path from A reaches B
through proofs, steps and equations only (definitions are vocabulary and are not passed through).
55 results, 78 direct dependencies (43 after transitive reduction). The slide version is `results_view.json`:
the 27 results Theorem 1 rests on and their 49 direct dependencies (29 not implied by others), each edge with a note
where it is method reuse or inferred.

The skeleton (numbered results, direct dependencies):

- **Theorem 1** (no denser packing than E8 in R^8) <- **Theorem 2** (Cohn-Elkies LP bound, cited) + **Theorem 3** (an optimal
  g exists). The whole proof of Theorem 1 is one sentence: "Theorem 2 applied to the optimal function f(x)=g(sqrt2 x)
  immediately implies Theorem 1."
- **Theorem 3** <- the proof of Theorems 3 and 4 (one proof, three parts (g1), (g2), (g3)) <- Propositions 2 and 6 (sin^2
  factorisations: double zeroes of a, b) for (g1); Propositions 3 and 7 (Laplace representations for all r >= 0) for (g2);
  Propositions 4 and 8 (values at 0) for (g3); plus the Hejhal/Bruinier coefficient bounds and the interval-arithmetic check.
- Propositions 1 and 5 (a, b are Schwartz Fourier eigenfunctions, eigenvalues +1 and -1) are **never cited** in the final
  proof; the graph has them only through two edges marked `basis: inferred` (g-hat = (pi i/8640) a - (i/240 pi) b needs
  F(a)=a, F(b)=-b). Without the inference they would be dead ends. The Lean blueprint states exactly these two
  dependencies (`thm:g1` uses `prop:a-fourier`, `prop:b-fourier`).

Depth: the longest chain of results into Theorem 1 has **7 steps**:
Gamma(1) is generated by S, T -> action of S, T on theta^4 -> h in M^!_{-2}(Gamma_0(2)) -> Proposition 6 -> Proposition 7 ->
Proposition 8 -> Theorem 3 -> Theorem 1. Among numbered results only: depth 5 (Prop 2 -> Prop 3 -> Prop 7 -> Prop 8 -> Thm 3 -> Thm 1;
the Prop 3 -> Prop 7 link is "the proof is analogous to the proof of Proposition 3"). Through every node type (definitions
included) the longest path is 18, starting at the upper half-plane.

Most-used results (direct dependents in the results view / all results downstream):
first q-coefficients of phi_{-4}, phi_{-2}, phi_0 (5 / 16); Rademacher-Petersson formula for c_j(n), cited (5 / 12);
Proposition 3 (5 / 11); transformation rule of phi_0 under z -> -1/z (4 / 13); Hejhal-Bruinier convergent expansions, cited
(4 / 11); q-expansions of psi_I, psi_T, psi_S (4 / 10); Proposition 7 (4 / 8). Theorem 2 has only 3 direct dependents but
18 results downstream. Most-used nodes overall: Fourier transform (16 direct users), radial-profile notation f_0(r) (12), the
phi definitions (12), a (10), sphere packing (9).

## 2. Where the "magic" functions enter

Six definitions in Section 4 carry the construction: varphi_{-2}, varphi_{-4} (weakly holomorphic, from E_4, E_6);
**phi_{-4}, phi_{-2}, phi_0** (quasimodular, via E_2: 12 direct users, 35 Level-1 nodes downstream); **a** (contour
integrals of phi_0: 10 direct users, 29 downstream); **h = 128(theta_00^4+theta_01^4)/theta_10^8** (4 / 27);
**psi_I, psi_T, psi_S** (9 / 24); **b** (9 / 21). Each reaches Theorem 1. Of the 26 results Theorem 1 rests on, 16 are
downstream of the magic definitions; the other 10 are the cited Cohn-Elkies theorem, the "f may be taken radial" reduction and
modular-forms background (E_2 transformation law, theta transformation laws, generators of Gamma(1), chain rule and slash
action, Rademacher, Hejhal-Bruinier, effective coefficient bounds). The paper motivates the construction in one remark
(RP2, "This property gives us a hint on constructing the function g explicitly", a `mention` edge from the double-zero claim):
the heuristic is in the graph but carries no logical weight.

17 concepts are shared; the LLM graph adds 9 (holomorphic function, contour integral, limit superior, ...), the rules graph adds

LLM concepts by number of paper nodes grounded to them: Fourier series and Fourier coefficients (9), Absolutely convergent

Upper half-plane, Modified Bessel Function (2 each), then one each: Lebesgue measure, Open ball, Limit superior, **Kepler's

Linear fractional transformation, Finite dimension, Even integer, Fourth power, Closed contour integral, Double integral,

**What the layer sees: the analysis the proof is written in, not the objects it is about.**
- The framework grounds: Lebesgue measure and open balls (DP1, DP2), the Fourier transform and Fourier coefficients,
  absolute convergence (the q-expansion arguments), the upper half-plane, linear fractional transformations (the action of
  SL_2(Z)), contour integrals, the modified Bessel function in the Rademacher-Petersson formula (TP10), and the Poisson
  summation formula, which is exactly the step (PP4) by which the Cohn-Elkies bound forces the zeroes of g at lattice points.
  The two grounded *results* are Poisson summation and the Kepler conjecture (TP2).
- **Nothing specific to the proof grounds.** The layer has no concept at all for sphere packing, packing density, the E8
  lattice (or any root lattice), modular form, quasimodular form, weakly holomorphic form, the modular group Gamma(1) /
  Gamma(2) / Gamma_0(2), q-expansion, Eisenstein series (only Eisenstein's criterion and Eisenstein integers), Laplace
  representations of radial functions, or interval arithmetic. The six "magic" definitions of Section 4 (varphi, phi, a, h,
  psi, b), the four numbered theorems and six of the eight propositions ground to nothing; the two that do (Props 3 and 7)
  ground only to "absolutely convergent series".
- **Where a concept exists, the term does not fire or fires on the wrong word.** The Schwartz class exists (C13812, term
  "schwartz class", one PDE book) but the paper says "Schwartz function", so DP11 grounds to *partial derivative* instead.
  Jacobi theta functions exist (ProofWiki C8230) but the paper writes theta_00, theta_01, theta_10, so the only theta node
  grounded (ClP17) lands on *Fourth Power*. "Holomorphic modular form" (DP24) and "weakly-holomorphic modular form" (DP30)
  ground to *Holomorphic Function*; "Eisenstein series E_k" (DP26) to *Even Integer*; the remark "g is a Laplace transform of
  a modular form" (RP3) to *Closed form*. The E8 lattice is only "lattice point".
- Grounding is 2:1 external over paper nodes (26 vs 14): the imported background grounds, the new mathematics does not.
  The rules graph, which has the same statement text, grounds the same vocabulary (17 of its 19 concepts are shared)
  with half the edges, since it has no proof steps or claims.

For the talk: the textbook layer places the proof in classical Fourier analysis and complex analysis, and it correctly
finds the one classical theorem that drives the LP bound (Poisson summation). It cannot see the modular-forms machinery
the proof is famous for, because the 30 books have no modular-forms chapter and ProofWiki's entries are under names the
paper does not use. A single textbook on modular forms (q-expansions, Eisenstein series, theta functions, the modular group)
would close most of the gap.

## 4. LLM vs rules

- **The top of the proof is inverted in the rules graph.** The one-sentence proof "Theorem 2 applied to ... implies Theorem 1"
  is attached by the parser to Theorem 3 (`DIST_PROOF2 proves T3`), and its \ref to Theorem 1 becomes a use: Theorem 1 ->
  proof -> Theorem 3. Nothing reaches Theorem 1 in the rules graph (one learned edge from the Kepler conjecture), against 26
  results in the LLM graph. The same happens one level down: the proof of Theorem 4 ends "this finishes the proof of
  Theorems 4 and 3", read as Theorem 3 -> proof of Theorem 4.
- Dependencies among numbered results (by \label): LLM 31, rules 12, 11 shared; the one rules-only pair is the inverted
  thm:main -> thm:g. The rules graph lacks every Proposition -> Theorem 3 dependency and Theorem 2/3 -> Theorem 1.
- Typing: the rules graph has the Hales theorem and the Kepler conjecture both as conjectures, the Jacobi identity, the
  minimal distance of E8 and "theta^4 in M_2(Gamma(2))" as definitions; 3 nodes marked cited (LLM: 78, with citations).
  No proof steps (LLM: 78 steps in 9 proofs: e.g. proof of Proposition 6, 8 steps; proof of Theorems 3-4, three
  claim-steps with 9, 7 and 0 children).
- The rules graph splits displays more finely (120 equations, 82 of them unnumbered) and has no open problems, problems or
  remarks; the LLM graph adds the history as typed nodes (Kepler conjecture `resolves` -> Hales; open problem "exact
  Delta_d for d > 3" `resolves` -> Theorem 1; the j-coefficient problem `resolves` -> Rademacher).

Blueprint (`thefundamentaltheor3m/Sphere-Packing-Lean`): 141 statements (54 lemmas, 34 definitions, 19 theorems, 17
propositions, 15 corollaries, 2 remarks), 95 proofs, 463 edges; 93 of its 105 results support `MainTheorem`; longest
chain 6 (inv-power-summable -> Poisson summation -> Cohn-Elkies periodic -> Cohn-Elkies general -> CE_Main ->
upper-bound-E8 -> MainTheorem). The paper has 12 numbered results: the formalisation is about 10x larger.

gold set differs per extractor):

Caveat: the alignment is automatic (number in the blueprint name, else statement similarity >= 0.5, one-to-one). By our
reading about half of the 82 LLM pairs are right (all numbered results are; wrong ones include `MainTheorem` paired with the
Cohn-Elkies numerical bound and `def: h` with the modular group), so the edge scores are a noisy lower bound.

Hand mapping of the paper's results to the blueprint, and what the formalisers made explicit (59 blueprint statements have
no LLM counterpart: 16 on packings, density and E8; 3 Fourier analysis; 20 modular forms; 10 in Section 7; 10 in Section 8):

- **Theorem 2 is cited in the paper, proved in the blueprint**: Poisson summation (`thm:Poisson-summation-formula`), summability
  of Schwartz functions over periodic sets, `thm:Cohn-Elkies-periodic` -> `thm:Cohn-Elkies-general`.
- **Theorem 1 becomes five nodes**: Cohn-Elkies for periodic packings (`theorem:CE_Main`), periodic packings suffice
  (`thm:periodic-packing-optimal`, with packing-density lemmas), `corollary:upper-bound-E8`, `MainTheorem`, and the E8 density
  pi^4/384 (`E8Packing-density`, with E8 being a discrete lattice of covolume 1), which the paper only asserts.
- **The interval-arithmetic step is replaced by a proof.** The paper checks A(t) < 0 and B(t) > 0 with truncated q-expansions,
  tail bounds and interval arithmetic (steps PSP67, PSP75, subclass `other`). The blueprint proves `prop:ineqA` / `prop:ineqB`
  analytically: phi_0 = F/Delta, psi_S = -G/(2 Delta) with new quasimodular forms F, G; modular linear differential equations
  for F, G; a positivity theorem for Serre derivatives; Q(t) strictly decreasing with Q(0+) = 18/pi^2. That brings in the
  discriminant Delta and its nonvanishing, the Dedekind eta function, Ramanujan's formulas for E_2', E_4', E_6', Serre
  derivatives (equivariance, product rule), theta derivatives and dimension formulas: none of it is in the paper.
- **Analytic estimates the paper waves through are explicit lemmas**: |phi_0| <= C e^{-2 pi Im z} and the phi_{-2},
  phi_{-4}, psi bounds, bounds on each of the six contour integrals I_1..I_6, J_1..J_6, "smooth with fast decay implies
  Schwartz". In the paper these are one line each ("Analogous estimates can be obtained for all derivatives").
- **Splits and trims**: Propositions 1 and 5 each become Schwartz + Fourier (`prop:a-schwartz`, `prop:a-fourier`, ...);
  Propositions 4 and 8 keep only a(0), b(0): the values at sqrt 2 are never used. Theorems 3 and 4 lose their "Moreover"
  clause (g, g-hat vanish only at ||x||^2 in 2Z): the paper's proof has no step for it and the blueprint does not state it.
  The uniqueness of the E8 packing among periodic packings (ClP4) is not formalised.
- The blueprint has its own cycle: `thm:g` uses `thm:g1` and `thm:g1` uses `thm:g`.

## 6. Three findings for the talk

1. **The proof is a short spine on a wide base.** Theorem 1 is one sentence on top of Cohn-Elkies + "an optimal g
   exists"; everything is in Theorem 3, reached by a 5-7 step chain of propositions whose inputs are the six "magic"
   definitions of Section 4 (16 of the 26 supporting results sit downstream of them). The graph makes visible which
   inputs are new (phi_0, h) and which are textbook background.
2. **The graph shows what the text leaves implicit, and the formalisers agree.** The final proof never cites
   Propositions 1 and 5 (the eigenfunction property, the heart of the construction): the LLM graph needs two `inferred`
   edges to connect them, and the blueprint has exactly those two dependencies. Proposition 8 has no proof at all; the
   "Moreover" clause of Theorems 3-4 has no step, and the blueprint drops it; a \eqref in the proof of Proposition 7 points
   at the a-identity instead of the b-identity.
3. **Formalising multiplied the statements ~10x and replaced the computer step.** 141 blueprint statements vs 12 numbered
   results: the cited Cohn-Elkies theorem gets proved, "density = pi^4/384" gets proved, and the interval-arithmetic positivity
   check of A(t), B(t) becomes an analytic proof via modular differential equations and Serre derivatives. Against that
   blueprint the LLM graph recovers dependencies at edge F1 0.43 (rules 0.34; the 2026-09 rules graph 0.03), and the rules
   graph inverts the top of the proof (Theorem 1 as a premise of Theorem 3).
