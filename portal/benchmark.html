Benchmark: dependency extraction against human-made gold

How well does an extractor recover the dependencies between the results of a paper? We score extractors against two kinds of dependency structure made by people:

  • Author diagrams (146 papers): figures in which a paper's authors drew how its results depend on each other. Found by searching the LaTeX source of all of arXiv; the extractor reads the paper with the diagram removed.
  • Lean blueprints (91 blueprints): the informal plans of Lean formalisation projects, where every statement lists the results it uses. The extractor reads the blueprint with these markers removed.

Node R is the share of the gold's statements found; edge precision, recall and F1 are over the dependencies between them, pooled over items, with 95% bootstrap intervals over items.

Author diagrams usually draw only part of a paper's dependencies, so their precision counts only edges between results the diagram contains. Blueprint precision is lower partly because blueprints declare dependencies inside empty or placeholder proofs, which no reader of the text can recover.
